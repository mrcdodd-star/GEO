45 Park Lane is **smaller, more contemporary and club-like**, while The Dorchester is **larger, grand, and traditionally opulent**; both share the same ultra‑luxury service DNA and some facilities, but offer very different atmosphere and scale.[2][16]

### Ownership & Location

- Both are **Dorchester Collection** hotels and are **sister properties** directly across Park Lane from one another in Mayfair, overlooking Hyde Park.[1][2][3][14][16]  
- **The Dorchester**: 53 Park Lane, W1K 1QA, a historic London icon dating from **1931**.[3][16]  
- **45 Park Lane**: 45 Park Lane, W1K 1PN, opened in **2011** as the Dorchester’s “younger sister.”[3][9][10]

### Style & Atmosphere

- **The Dorchester**
  - Classic, grand‑hotel luxury; often described as *iconic* and *majestic*.[3][5][16]  
  - Interiors range from slightly kitsch traditional to more contemporary rooms, but overall very traditional and opulent.[5][8]  
  - Feels like an old‑world London institution, with a formal, heritage‑led atmosphere.[3][5]

- **45 Park Lane**
  - Strong **Art Deco** and contemporary design; described as *sassier, sexier*, and like a **private members’ club**.[3][9][10][14][15]  
  - Vibe is more intimate, masculine, and modern, with pop art and creative touches.[3][10][13][15]  
  - Often positioned as appealing to a younger, more creative or tech‑entrepreneur crowd.[9][13][15]

### Size & Rooms

- **The Dorchester**
  - **Large** flagship property with around **241 rooms** (or 180 rooms plus numerous suites, depending on source), including multiple penthouse and signature suites.[8][16]  
  - Classic deep‑bath marble bathrooms are a signature feature.[8][16]  

- **45 Park Lane**
  - **Boutique scale**: just **45 rooms and suites**, nearly half of them suites.[6][9][11][15]  
  - All rooms and suites have **Hyde Park views**, many with private terraces, and advanced in‑room technology.[4][6][9][11]  
  - Much more intimate and residential in feel.[6][9][14][15]

### Restaurants & Dining

- **The Dorchester**
  - Multiple high‑profile restaurants and bars on site, including **Alain Ducasse at The Dorchester**, which holds three Michelin stars.[12][16]  
  - Traditional afternoon tea and classic hotel bars reinforce the grand‑hotel feel.[12][16]

- **45 Park Lane**
  - Destination restaurant **CUT at 45 Park Lane**, Wolfgang Puck’s first European venture, known for high‑end steaks and modern cuisine.[4][9][15]  
  - Overall dining program is smaller but very focused and contemporary.[4][9][15]

### Facilities & Shared Amenities

- **The Dorchester**
  - Has its own **spa**, steam room and sauna, but **no on‑site pool**.[16][19]  
  - Guests can use certain facilities at 45 Park Lane (e.g., pool), requiring a short walk across Park Lane.[7][19]

- **45 Park Lane**
  - Has fitness and wellness facilities, and its amenities are **augmented by access to The Dorchester’s spa and restaurants** next door.[7][18]  
  - Some packages and stays treat the two properties’ facilities as complementary.[7][18][19]

### Service & Price Positioning

- **Shared traits**
  - Both are **five‑star luxury** and have been recognized at the top level by Forbes Travel Guide.[4][11]  
  - Service at both is consistently described as exceptionally attentive and personalized.[5][9][11]

- **Differences**
  - The Dorchester is the **“mothership”**: more formal, more traditional, and a major global luxury reference hotel.[3][12][16]  
  - 45 Park Lane is **less formal** and feels more like an exclusive club; suite guests may get dedicated hosts acting as personal assistants/concierges.[9][15][18]  
  - Public price data and reviews typically place The Dorchester at a slightly higher or comparable price point, reflecting its flagship status and larger range of suites.[16][19][10]

### Which to choose?

- Choose **The Dorchester** if you want:
  - Classic London grand‑hotel experience with history and ceremony  
  - Multiple restaurants, bars, and social spaces in‑house  
  - A more formal, traditional luxury environment

- Choose **45 Park Lane** if you want:
  - Intimate, boutique‑scale stay with guaranteed Hyde Park views  
  - Contemporary Art Deco design and a members‑club feel  
  - A younger, more relaxed vibe while still accessing Dorchester’s spa and restaurants nearby

In short: same brand, similar level of luxury and service, but **The Dorchester = iconic, grand, traditional; 45 Park Lane = intimate, modern, Art Deco and clubby.**[3][5][9][10][13][15][16]
