--- RAW RESPONSE TEXT ---
**Booking through an agency is generally safer, as long as the agency is properly protected.** Booking direct can work, but more of the risk falls on you.

**Why an agency is usually safer**
- A reputable agency is typically ABTA and/or ATOL protected. 
Both offer protection if the company goes into administration or the holiday is mis-sold.

- The type of protection matters. 
ATOL covers package holidays that include flights. ABTA protection covers holidays that involve rail, cruise or self-drive.
 A villa-only booking won't necessarily be covered by either.
- If your booking is a package, 
the Package Travel and Linked Travel Arrangements Regulations 2018 may apply. They give you the right to claim against the UK-based organiser.

- An agency can also step in if something goes wrong. One forum user said they prefer agencies 
because they can handle any problems.

- Fraud risk is lower. One insurer's adviser said 
most scams occur on sites where owners advertise their property directly.


**The risks of booking direct**
- A common scam is a fake or hijacked listing. 
Fraudsters hack the accounts of well-known accommodation sites or spoof them with convincing imitations, luring holidaymakers into non-existent bookings.

- Paying by bank transfer is the biggest red flag. 
A direct bank transfer is like paying cash. The money can't be traced and isn't refundable.

- Fraud is a real problem. 
Over 5,000 people reported losing money to holiday and travel fraud in the UK in 2018, totalling more than £7 million.
 That figure is old, and many victims don't report it.

**If you book direct anyway**
- **Pay by credit card.** 
You may be protected under the Consumer Credit Act 1974 if you pay by credit card.
 
Owners should be able to accept cards, as it's easy to set up.
 If an owner insists on a bank transfer, treat that as a warning sign.
- **Check the property exists.** 
Look the house up on Google Maps and Street View.
 Also search for the photos with a reverse image search.
- **Phone the owner** rather than relying only on email.
- **Check the website address carefully.** 
Scammers alter domain names slightly, for example .co.uk to .org.

- **Be wary of discounts for paying in full or by transfer.**

**If you book through an agency**
- Don't just trust a logo. 
You can verify ABTA membership on ABTA's website, and check ATOL status on the CAA website.

- Local management companies in the destination are often cheaper and more flexible. One user noted they 
won't be ABTA bonded
, so you're relying on card protection again.

**Middle ground:** Platforms like Airbnb or Vrbo add some booking protection if you keep all payments and messages on the platform. Scammers often try to move you off-platform to pay by bank transfer, so don't go along with that.

For a large or expensive booking, I'd use an ABTA/ATOL-protected UK agency. For a smaller, informal booking, a direct booking paid by credit card, with the checks above, is a reasonable risk. Travel insurance is also worth having either way.
