### What differentiates them in practice

#### 1. Rooms and design
- **The Peninsula** is widely praised for **room size and thoughtful design**: entry‑level rooms are roughly 47–51 m², closer to suites at older Mayfair grands, with calm palettes, floor‑to‑ceiling windows and very high‑spec tech and bathrooms.[4][10][15][18]  
- **The Dorchester** has historically had smaller, more mixed rooms, but the recent redesign by Pierre‑Yves Rochon delivers brighter, garden‑inspired interiors, new marble bathrooms and upgraded corridors.[4][5][19] Park‑view rooms are the ones to aim for.[4]

If **space and “newness” matter most**, the Peninsula wins clearly.[4][18]  
If you like **characterful, classic interiors with a heritage story**, the modernized Dorchester may appeal more.[2][5][19]

#### 2. Service culture
- The Peninsula brand is known for **meticulous, anticipatory service with Asian hospitality cues**: dedicated transport fleet, polished arrival, high staff‑to‑guest ratio.[5][14][15]  
- Recent professional reviews and ratings tend to place the Peninsula slightly ahead on consistency: it earned **three MICHELIN Keys**, versus two for The Dorchester.[2][20]  
- Guest feedback on The Dorchester is more mixed: some report excellent service, others note staff who felt “bothered to help” compared with The Peninsula and Berkeley.[3][12]

If **flawless, highly choreographed service is your top priority**, Peninsula is safer. If you’re comfortable with a more traditional, sometimes somewhat formal British grand‑hotel style, **Dorchester can still be very good**, especially post‑refresh.[2][4][5]

#### 3. Food and bars
- **The Dorchester**:
  - **Alain Ducasse at The Dorchester** (fine dining, French)[1][4][5]  
  - The Grill, China Tang, The Promenade (iconic afternoon tea), Vesper Bar, Artists’ Bar, The Spatisserie.[1][4][5]  
  This is arguably one of the richest on‑property dining ecosystems in London, with classic and modern options.
- **The Peninsula**:
  - **Brooklands by Claude Bosi**, a Michelin‑starred rooftop restaurant and bar themed around British aviation/automotive; often singled out as a destination in its own right.[1][4][14]  
  - Canton Blue (design‑heavy, Chinese fine dining), Little Blue bar, The Lobby, Peninsula Boutique & Café.[1][5]

If you want **classic London institutions like afternoon tea and long‑established venues**, Dorchester is stronger.[4][5]  
If you want **cutting‑edge, design‑led Michelin dining on site**, Peninsula’s Brooklands gives it an edge.[4][14]

#### 4. Spa & wellness
- **Peninsula**: its spa and pool are repeatedly described as **among the best new hotel wellness facilities in London**, with Thai‑inspired treatments and an ozone‑filtered indoor pool.[4][5]  
- **Dorchester**: the refurbishment has significantly upgraded its spa; one expert guide notes that it is now “genuinely competitive with the Peninsula’s.”[4]

If spa is central to your stay, **Peninsula is still slightly ahead**, but the gap is now much smaller, and **Dorchester’s new spa is strong**.[4][5]

#### 5. Location nuances
Both are in central, extremely convenient areas by Hyde Park:

- **Dorchester (Mayfair, Park Lane)**: classic luxury district; perfect for **Bond Street, Mayfair restaurants, and Hyde Park walks**, with a sense of history and a more “name‑brand” London glamour.[1][4][5][11][19]  
- **Peninsula (Belgravia, Hyde Park Corner)**: slightly quieter and more residential; steps from **Knightsbridge, Buckingham Palace area, Green Park**, and sits at a grand corner by Wellington Arch.[1][7][9][15]

Location isn’t a deciding factor unless you prefer:
- **Mayfair vibe and long‑established London glamour → Dorchester**.  
- **Belgravia’s slightly softer, residential atmosphere → Peninsula**.

### Price and value

- Most recent guides quote **starting rates** around:
  - Dorchester: roughly **£600–£800+** for standard rooms, sometimes higher after the refresh.[1][11][19]  
  - Peninsula: often **£880–£1,000+** per night for entry‑level rooms.[1][7][11][18]

You are paying a premium at The Peninsula for:
- Larger, brand‑new rooms
- Top‑tier service and spa
- Highest current Michelin Key rating (three Keys)[2][20]

Dorchester offers:
- More *heritage per pound* and a slightly lower entry price
- Very strong dining and public spaces (Promenade, Vesper Bar) that feel quintessentially London.[4][5][19]

### Which should you choose?

**Pick The Peninsula London if:**
- You want **the newest hardware** (rooms, spa, tech) and very large standard rooms.[4][10][18]  
- You prioritize **service quality and a calm, contemporary aesthetic**.[4][5][14]  
- You’re willing to pay a premium and like the idea of a “future‑classic” rather than an old icon.[2][20]

**Pick The Dorchester if:**
- You want **classic London glamour and history**, in the heart of Mayfair.[1][4][5][19]  
- You care about **iconic London dining and social spaces** (tea at The Promenade, Alain Ducasse, China Tang).[1][4][5]  
- You prefer a slightly **more traditional grand‑hotel atmosphere** and may appreciate marginally lower rates.[4][11][19]
