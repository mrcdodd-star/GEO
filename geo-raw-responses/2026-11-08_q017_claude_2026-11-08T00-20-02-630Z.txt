--- RAW RESPONSE TEXT ---
**An agency is generally the safer option, but you pay for that, and booking direct can be done safely if you take precautions.**

## Why agencies are safer
- **Financial protection.** 
Package holidays that include flights plus accommodation and/or car hire must be protected under an ATOL licence.
 A villa-only booking with a UK agency 
typically falls under ABTA's trust-account protection rather than ATOL, unless flights are bundled in.
 Check that the agency actually holds this cover, because the rules differ by booking type.
- **Someone to turn to.** 
Agents have physically inspected properties and give you a point of contact if something goes wrong.

- **Less fraud risk.** 
Action Fraud reported more than £15m lost to holiday booking scams between April 2022 and April 2023.
 Fake villa listings are a common type.

## The trade-offs of booking direct
- **Cost.** Direct bookings can 
save 10-15% but carry more risk.
 One source puts the saving at 10-20%, and another puts agency commission at 15-25%. Those figures come from agency and villa-related sites, so treat them as rough estimates.
- **Less recourse.** 
Direct from a French owner, no UK protection applies and disputes run through French civil courts.
 That example is for France, but the same logic applies across Europe.
- **Cancellations.** If an owner cancels, you may be left to find and pay for a replacement yourself.

Most of the sources I found are agencies or travel businesses, so they have an interest in recommending agencies. The ATOL and ABTA protections are real, though, and direct bookings don't get them.

## If you book direct
- **Pay by credit card.** 
Paying by credit card offers the most comprehensive protection from fraud.
 In the UK, Section 75 can apply to purchases over £100. Be wary of anyone who insists on a bank transfer.
- **Check the property exists.** Look at it on Google Maps and Street View. Search for the photos with a reverse image search, and check that the owner's details match the listing.
- **Phone the owner.** 
A legitimate owner will understand the caution; a scammer will not.

- **Get a written contract** with terms and conditions. 
If you're asked to pay without signing anything, the transaction lacks basic consumer protection.

- **Be careful on platforms.** Scammers often try to move you off Airbnb, Vrbo or Booking.com to pay by transfer. Pay inside the platform to keep its protection.
- **Check the agency's membership.** 
Search the ATOL or ABTA sites using the licence number listed on the company's site.
 Scammers can fake the logos.
- **Take out travel insurance** with cancellation cover.

If the villa is expensive, or you're booking months ahead, an agency is usually worth the extra cost. If you do book direct, use a credit card and check the owner is genuine first.
