--- RAW RESPONSE TEXT ---
**Usually, booking through a reputable UK-based villa agency is safer** than paying an owner directly: a good agency can verify the property, provide support if something goes wrong, and may offer financial protection. For the strongest protection, consider a villa as part of a package holiday with a UK tour operator; check exactly what protection applies, because not every agency booking is protected.[3]

Direct booking can still be safe, but it puts more responsibility on you to verify the owner and property and check the contract and cancellation terms.[4] If you book direct:

- Verify the property exists and check that its photos and details aren’t copied from another listing.
- Speak to the owner using a phone number you’ve independently checked, and get the dates, total price, deposit and refund terms in writing.
- Pay by credit card if possible. For eligible purchases costing £100–£30,000, Section 75 may make your card provider jointly liable if something goes wrong; paying by bank transfer offers much less recourse.[2][4]
- Don’t pay by cash, Western Union or MoneyGram, and be wary of pressure to pay quickly or of a price that seems unusually low.[2][4]

**Bottom line:** choose a reputable agency if peace of mind matters most. A direct booking may be reasonable after careful checks, especially if you can pay by credit card.
