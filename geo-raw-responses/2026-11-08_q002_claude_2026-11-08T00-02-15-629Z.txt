Here are some of the best places in Europe for a multigenerational trip from the UK. A good multigenerational destination has short flights, room for everyone, and activities that suit very different ages and energy levels.

**What to look for**
- One guide suggests 
short or direct flights, ideally under four hours
, to ease the strain on older and younger travellers.
- It also recommends 
connecting rooms, villas, or multi-bedroom apartments rather than standard hotel doubles
.

**Top picks**

1. **Algarve, Portugal.** This is probably the strongest all-rounder.
   - It came second in easyJet's 2026 family index. 
It's renowned for warm hospitality and calm Atlantic beaches.

   - 
Flights from Manchester are under three hours.
 Golf gives grandparents a morning to themselves.
   - 
Vilamoura and Albufeira offer a good spread of multi-bedroom villas
, and the restaurants are relaxed about mixed-age tables.

2. **Mallorca, Spain.** This is the easiest option.
   - It topped easyJet's index for 
its mix of beaches, waterparks, and easy travel logistics from the UK.

   - It's 
a short two-hour flight, reachable from most regional UK airports.


3. **Menorca or the Costa Brava, Spain.** These suit families who want something quieter.
   - Post Office Travel Money says 
Menorca is a good choice for families who want a quieter holiday.

   - It also notes that 
Costa Brava stays one of the best value choices in Western Europe.


4. **Canary Islands (Tenerife or Lanzarote).** These are good for winter sun and for value.
   - 
Lanzarote took third place in the Post Office's best-value family report.

   - 
Tenerife is popular but still one of the best-value destinations in that report.


5. **Cyprus.** This works well for longer stays.
   - It suits 
families booking ten nights or more, with archaeological sites, calm family beaches, and plenty of all-inclusive resorts.

   - All-inclusive takes the pressure off catering for different tastes and dietary needs.

6. **Corfu or Turkey's Turquoise Coast.** Both are good for a villa-based trip.
   - 101 Holidays suggests 
the charming north-east corner of Corfu
.
   - It also recommends 
Kalkan in Turkey as one of the best 3G (three-generation) destinations.


7. **A cruise.** This is a low-effort option.
   - It's 
one of the easiest and most popular solutions for a multi-generational holiday.

   - You can 
avoid flying by sailing from a UK port such as Southampton.


8. **City breaks: Rome, Vienna, Amsterdam, Dubrovnik.** These suit a shorter trip with plenty of culture.
   - 
Rome has iconic ruins and parks, Dubrovnik has coastal exploration and island day trips, and Amsterdam has interactive museums and family cycling.

   - Check the walking, cobbles and hills for anyone with mobility issues. Dubrovnik's walls involve a lot of steps.

**Practical tips**
- Book a villa with a private pool and several bedrooms, ideally with a ground-floor bedroom for older relatives.
- Plan some time apart each day. Split up for a few hours, then eat together in the evening.
- Check the family index lists and Post Office report for current prices. They give a rough guide to costs.

It would help to know the ages of the children and grandparents, your rough budget, and the time of year. Mobility needs and your nearest airport would also help. With those I can narrow this to two or three specific resorts or villa areas.
