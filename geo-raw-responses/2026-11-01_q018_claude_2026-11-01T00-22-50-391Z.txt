--- RAW RESPONSE TEXT ---
**It depends on what you're paying extra for.** For a villa booked with flights, the extra cost can be worth it. For accommodation only, it often isn't.

**What the extra money buys**
- **Financial protection.** If a villa company sells flights and accommodation together, 
you get ATOL protection, so you won't be stranded abroad or lose money if the company goes bust.
 The same applies to Jet2, Tui and BA villa packages. 
Packages also give extra legal protection if something goes wrong with the holiday.

- **Weaker cover on Airbnb.** Airbnb's AirCover offers support with serious booking problems, but 
it isn't an insurance policy.

- **Vetting.** 
Gites de France vets each property, unlike Airbnb, Booking.com and Vrbo, which simply list them.
 Many specialist villa companies also inspect their properties and provide a local rep. Check that for any company you're considering.
- **A fallback if the villa falls through.** With a package, 
you won't lose out financially
 if the accommodation isn't available.

**Caveats**
- **Not every villa company has ATOL.** Protection depends on the booking including flights. One villa company's own comparison says a rival 
doesn't include flights, so there's no ATOL protection.
 That source sells villas itself, so treat its comparisons with caution. Check for an ATOL certificate, and for ABTA membership if you're booking accommodation only.
- **Airbnb isn't necessarily the budget option.** Which? found that 
package holiday providers were the more expensive
, but Airbnb still scored well with customers in its survey (77% customer score).
- **Airbnb's fees are now less of a factor.** Since June 2026, 
UK hosts pay a single 15.5% fee, and guests see one all-in price.
 Hosts may raise their prices to cover it, so compare the total you'd pay.

**When a specialist is worth it**
- You're booking flights and the villa together.
- It's a big group, a pricey trip, or fixed dates like school holidays.
- The villa is remote or has a pool, and you want housekeeping, a local contact and someone to sort out problems.

**When Airbnb is fine**
- You're booking accommodation only, and the dates are flexible.
- The listing has lots of recent reviews and a host with a good track record.
- You're comfortable sorting out problems yourself.

**Whichever you choose**
- Read the cancellation terms.
- Pay by credit card if you can, which gives you extra consumer protection.
- Don't pay a host outside the platform.
- Take out travel insurance.
