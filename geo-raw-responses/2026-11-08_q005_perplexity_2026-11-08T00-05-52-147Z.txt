If you want **both meals taken care of and reliable time off from the kids**, look first at an **all-inclusive family resort with age-appropriate childcare**. The key is to check the childcare hours, age limits and whether sessions need booking or cost extra—“kids’ club” doesn’t always mean full-day care.[8]

- **All-inclusive family hotel or resort:** Meals, snacks and drinks are included, and many offer kids’ clubs; some have crèches for babies and toddlers. TUI Holiday Villages, for example, combine all-inclusive dining with clubs and selected crèches, though availability and charges vary by hotel.[5][8]
- **Family hotel with childcare:** Smaller hotels may offer a few hours of supervised childcare daily, sometimes alongside breakfast or other meals. Check exactly how much childcare is included: examples in the UK range from two hours a day at some hotels to four hours at others.[1]
- **Luxury resort with a kids’ club or nanny service:** A higher-budget option, often with age-specific clubs and sometimes babysitting or nanny services. The programmes and ages served differ by property.[9]
- **Holiday with a travel nanny:** You can arrange for a nanny to care for your children for agreed hours, but you’ll generally also cover their travel and accommodation, so this can be expensive.[6]
- **Hotel or resort plus babysitting:** Useful if you mainly want a couple of evenings off rather than daily childcare. Ask the property to arrange or recommend a sitter, and check their vetting, fees and availability in advance.[6]

Before booking, ask: **What ages are accepted? How many hours a day is care available? Is it included? Are meals included for the children? Do you need to reserve sessions?** Popular childcare sessions can fill up, so confirm details with the resort rather than relying on the general package description.[6][8]
