If you want the **more classic grand London hotel**, choose **The Dorchester**; if you want the **better Hyde Park-side location, spa, and a slightly calmer feel**, choose **Mandarin Oriental Hyde Park**.[9][10][3]

- **The Dorchester** is on **Park Lane in Mayfair**, directly opposite Hyde Park, and is widely described as the more traditional, iconic grand hotel with a strong food-and-beverage reputation, including **Alain Ducasse at The Dorchester**.[18][10][13]
- **Mandarin Oriental Hyde Park** is at **66 Knightsbridge**, directly on the park edge, and is described as especially strong for **park views**, **spa**, and a polished luxury experience.[4][3][10][14]

A quick comparison:

| Category | The Dorchester | Mandarin Oriental Hyde Park |
|---|---|---|
| **Style** | Classic, formal, iconic grand hotel[10][18] | Modern-luxury, polished, calm[10][2] |
| **Location** | Park Lane / Mayfair, opposite Hyde Park[18][13] | Knightsbridge, directly by Hyde Park[4][14] |
| **Best for** | Dining, heritage, classic London atmosphere[3][10] | Spa, park views, quieter luxury[3][10] |
| **Dining** | Alain Ducasse at The Dorchester is a standout, with three Michelin stars noted in sources[10][18] | Dinner by Heston Blumenthal and Bar Boulud are major draws[10] |
| **Room count** | About **180 rooms** and **57 suites** are listed in one source[5] | About **168 rooms** and **26 suites** are listed in one source[5] |

If your priority is **afternoon tea, a more ceremonial five-star feel, and top-tier restaurant prestige**, The Dorchester has the edge.[10][18] If your priority is **direct park proximity, spa quality, and a slightly more relaxed stay**, Mandarin Oriental Hyde Park is the stronger pick.[3][10]
