### Key Advice for Americans Renting a Villa in Italy

1. **Verify Air Conditioning:** Standard central AC is uncommon in older Italian country villas. If summer heat is a dealbreaker, ensure the agency specifically confirms that bedrooms have air conditioning (not just portable fans).
2. **Saturday-to-Saturday Rules:** Many traditional Italian villas—especially in Tuscany and Umbria—still require 7-night minimum stays running strictly from Saturday to Saturday during peak season (June through September).
3. **Road Access & ZTLs:** Rural Italian villas often require driving up winding, narrow gravel roads (*strade bianche*). If you plan to rent an SUV or minivans for large groups, confirm with your agency that the villa's driveway and parking can accommodate larger vehicles.
4. **Local Tourist Taxes (*Tassa di Soggiorno*):** Most Italian municipalities charge a minor cash tourist tax per person, per night (usually €1–€5). Agencies usually require this to be paid in cash in Euros to the house manager upon arrival.
