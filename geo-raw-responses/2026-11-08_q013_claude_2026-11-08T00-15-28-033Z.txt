Here's a checklist for booking a luxury villa abroad from the UK.

## 1. Booking protection
- **Check who you're paying.** Booking through a established agency or platform usually gives you more recourse than paying an owner directly by bank transfer. Bank transfers to individuals are a common route for fraud.
- **Pay by credit card where you can.** Section 75 of the Consumer Credit Act can make your card provider jointly liable for purchases between £100 and £30,000. Debit cards offer chargeback, which is weaker.
- **Package or villa-only?** If the villa is bundled with flights, the booking is more likely to be a protected package. Look for ATOL protection (flights) or ABTA membership. A villa-only booking usually has no such protection, so travel insurance matters more.
- **Read the cancellation terms.** Look at the deposit, the balance due date, and what happens if you cancel or the owner does. Luxury villas often want 30–50% upfront and the balance 8–12 weeks before arrival.

## 2. Verify the property is real
- Look for reviews on independent sites, not just the listing's own.
- Do a reverse image search on the photos.
- Ask for a video call or a recent video walkthrough.
- Check the address on satellite maps for proximity to roads, building sites, or other properties.
- Ask for the tourist rental licence number. Many places require one, including Spain, Portugal, Italy, Greece and Croatia.

## 3. What's actually included
- **Staff:** ask whether a housekeeper, chef, concierge or pool person is included, and whether that's daily or occasional.
- **Extras:** pool heating, air-con, electricity, and cleaning can all be charged on top. Ask if there's a damage deposit and how and when it's returned.
- **Pool:** check whether it's heated, whether it's fenced or gated (important with children), and whether it's private or shared.
- **Facilities:** check Wi-Fi speed if anyone will be working, the bed and bathroom setup, and the kitchen equipment.
- **Accessibility:** ask about stairs, steep driveways and road access.

## 4. Location and logistics
- Check the transfer time from the airport and which UK airports fly there. Direct flights make a big difference.
- Find out if you need a car, and how far the nearest shop, restaurant or beach is.
- Check whether the villa is in a quiet area, and whether there's any construction nearby.
- Think about season. Some places have a short window of good weather, and pools may be unheated in shoulder months.

## 5. Entry rules for UK travellers
If you're going to Europe:
- **EES:** 
The Entry/Exit System is a biometric border log. It has been live since October 2025 and fully operational since 10 April 2026.
 Expect fingerprint and photo registration on your first entry, and allow extra time at the airport.
- **ETIAS:** 
As of 26 October 2026, no ETIAS start date has been announced.
 
Any website currently claiming to process ETIAS applications for UK citizens is a scam.
 The EU has said it will give several months' notice before launch, so check again before you book a trip for 2027.
- **Passport validity:** check the current rules on how old your passport can be and how much validity it needs. Check gov.uk's foreign travel advice for your destination.
- **90/180 rule:** this applies if you're staying longer in the Schengen area.

## 6. Insurance and money
- Take out travel insurance that covers cancellation, medical care and, ideally, the villa's cost if a booking fails. Check that its cover limits are enough for a high-value rental.
- Take a UK GHIC for EU countries, but don't rely on it instead of insurance.
- Use a card with no foreign transaction fees, and pay in local currency to avoid poor exchange rates.
- Check whether tourist tax is payable on arrival.

## 7. Questions to ask before paying
1. Is the full price confirmed in writing, including all fees?
2. What's the damage deposit, and how is it returned?
3. What happens if the villa is unavailable, or the pool or air-con breaks?
4. Is there a local contact or property manager I can reach during the stay?
5. What are the check-in and check-out times, and are there any house rules (events, noise, numbers of guests)?
