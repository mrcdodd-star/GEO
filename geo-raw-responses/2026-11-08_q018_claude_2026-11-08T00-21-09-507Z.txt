**Sometimes, but not automatically.** It depends on what you're buying and how much hassle you'll tolerate if something goes wrong.

**What you get from a specialist**
- **Vetting.** 
Which? notes that each property is vetted, unlike sites such as Airbnb, Booking.com and Vrbo, which simply list them.
 One example is Villa Plus, where 
experts visit each villa five times before adding it to the collection.
 Another review says 
Airbnb and Vrbo quality varies more widely.

- **Support on the ground.** Jet2Villas, for example, says its team 
ensures all villas are spotless on arrival and coordinates in-resort support.
 That helps most if the pool pump breaks or the host goes quiet.
- **Bundling.** 
Packages can include flights, villa, car hire and luggage in one place, so the faff level is low.
 Package bookings with flights usually come with ATOL or package travel protection. Accommodation-only bookings on Airbnb generally don't. Check the terms on any booking.

**Where the premium may not be worth it**
- **Customer satisfaction is similar.** In the Which? survey, Airbnb scored 77% from 907 respondents. Villa Plus, a luxury specialist, scored 74% from 54 respondents. The Villa Plus sample is small, so I wouldn't read much into the gap. Still, Airbnb isn't obviously worse in the ratings.
- **Airbnb isn't always the cheapest.** 
Guests are typically charged 5–15% of the reservation subtotal
 in fees. One owner on a forum said their Airbnb price was about 15% higher than booking direct through their local manager's site. Searching for the property's own website or checking Booking.com or Vrbo can save money.
- **Choice.** Airbnb has far more listings, including quirky and budget options. Specialists tend to have fewer properties, all curated.

**Rough rule of thumb**
- A specialist is worth the extra if you're taking a **family or group trip, spending a lot, going somewhere remote, or want a pool and facilities you can rely on**. It also helps if you want flights, car hire or childcare arranged for you.
- Airbnb or Vrbo is fine if you're **comfortable vetting listings yourself**, such as reading recent reviews and checking host response times. It also suits you if you want to **find a bargain**, or if you're booking somewhere with plenty of well-reviewed properties.

**Middle ground:** curated platforms like Plum Guide, or specialists such as Simpson Travel, James Villas, Oliver's Travels and CV Villas. Several of these are UK-based, so UK consumer protections are easier to rely on.
