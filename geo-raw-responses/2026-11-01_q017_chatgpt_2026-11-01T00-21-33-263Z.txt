Here are some pros, cons, and safety tips to help you decide whether it’s safer to book a villa through an agency or directly with the owner—particularly from the UK. Ultimately, there’s no one-size-fits-all answer; the safest choice depends on circumstances and the safeguards you put in place.

## What protections exist under UK law

- If you book accommodation in the UK or abroad via a **travel agent** or agency, sometimes **Section 75 of the Consumer Credit Act 1974** applies. If the accommodation provider or travel company doesn’t deliver as promised, your credit card company may be jointly liable. ([ukfinance.org.uk](https://www.ukfinance.org.uk/faqs-chargeback-rights-and-section-75?utm_source=openai))  
- **Consumer Rights Act 2015** requires any service you pay for (including holiday lets) to be carried out with reasonable care and skill. If the villa is not as described, you have rights against the party you made the contract with. ([consumerrightsexpert.co.uk](https://www.consumerrightsexpert.co.uk/holiday-lets-contract-with-owner-agent.html?utm_source=openai))  
- Where you book via a platform or agency, whether that platform is merely listing properties (an intermediary) or actually selling you the stay determines who you can complain to and who is legally responsible. ([consumerrightsexpert.co.uk](https://www.consumerrightsexpert.co.uk/holiday-lets-contract-with-owner-agent.html?utm_source=openai))

## Booking through an agency: what you get, what could go wrong

**Pros:**

- Agencies typically handle many things: vetting the property, handling check-ins, mediating disputes.  
- Payment systems often more secure; many agencies accept credit/debit cards and sometimes offer additional financial protection (e.g. bonds or insurance). ([thevillaagency.co.uk](https://thevillaagency.co.uk/booking-conditions/?utm_source=openai))  
- Easier to enforce your rights: if the agency is registered, or part of a trade body (e.g. ABTA or similar), you have more routes for complaints or compensation. ([thevillaagency.co.uk](https://thevillaagency.co.uk/booking-conditions/?utm_source=openai))  

**Cons:**

- More expensive: agencies charge fees or take commissions, so cost can be higher than booking directly.  
- Sometimes less flexibility: cancellation policies and other terms may be stricter.  
- You may still end up in a contract with the villa owner directly, depending on how the agency operates—so not all agency bookings mean more protection. ([consumerrightsexpert.co.uk](https://www.consumerrightsexpert.co.uk/holiday-lets-contract-with-owner-agent.html?utm_source=openai))

## Booking directly with the owner: what to watch out for

**Pros:**

- Potentially cheaper: no agency commission, you might negotiate directly.  
- More flexibility in terms (e.g. cancellation, arrival times, bespoke arrangements).  

**Cons and risks:**

- **Less legal protection**: if you hire from a private individual, some consumer protection laws may not fully apply. ([ukicc.info](https://www.ukicc.info/topics/travel/accommodation/?utm_source=openai))  
- **Scams are more likely**: fake listings, nonexistent villas, or owners who ask you to pay off-platform (bank transfer, etc.) without protection. ([which.co.uk](https://www.which.co.uk/news/article/how-to-avoid-scams-when-booking-last-minute-holidays-a118r3A8SoIG?utm_source=openai))  
- **Payment method matters**: if you pay by bank transfer, you generally lose legal protections like Section 75. ([moneysavingexpert.com](https://www.moneysavingexpert.com/travel/cheap-holiday-rentals/?utm_source=openai))  
- **Dispute resolution harder**: with a private owner, resolving issues (cleanliness, amenities, non-arrival, etc.) can become more difficult if no formal agency or platform is involved.

## Key questions to ask, regardless of who you book with

1. **Who are you contracting with?**  
   Who took your money and signed the contract? The owner, the agency, or a platform? Knowing this determines who is legally liable. ([consumerrightsexpert.co.uk](https://www.consumerrightsexpert.co.uk/holiday-lets-contract-with-owner-agent.html?utm_source=openai))  

2. **What are the cancellation and refund rules?**  
   Are they clearly stated? Are there non-refundable deposits? How far in advance do you need to cancel, and what refund do you get?  

3. **Payment method**  
   Credit card gives more protection under Section 75 or via chargeback. Avoid payments via service like Western Union, instant money transfer, or being asked to move off-platform. ([moneysavingexpert.com](https://www.moneysavingexpert.com/travel/cheap-holiday-rentals/?utm_source=openai))  

4. **Reviews & legitimacy**  
   Read past guest reviews (especially recent ones). Check the owner or agency’s contact details. Look for red flags like brand-new listings, overly good-to-be-true pricing, or requests to pay “privately” or off-platform. ([which.co.uk](https://www.which.co.uk/news/article/how-to-avoid-scams-when-booking-last-minute-holidays-a118r3A8SoIG?utm_source=openai))  

5. **Written contract / booking confirmation**  
   Get everything in writing: property address, amenities, liability/deposit, house rules, check in/out, what happens in case of problems.

## Which tends to be “safer” overall (for most people)

If I had to provide a recommendation based on what most people are comfortable with, and aiming for *safety over savings*:

- For first-time bookings in unfamiliar locations, or when you’re spending a lot of money, an **agency or platform** with clearly stated protections tends to be safer.  
- If you choose to go direct, it’s vital to verify the owner and payment channels, and check exactly who you're contracting with.
