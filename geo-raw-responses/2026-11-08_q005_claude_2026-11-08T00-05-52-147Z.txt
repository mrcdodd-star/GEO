There are three main types of holiday that cover both. Which fits best depends on your children's ages, your budget and how far you want to travel.

## 1. All-inclusive resorts abroad
These give you the most "hands-off" holiday. Meals are included and kids' clubs run most of the day.

- **Mainstream package operators (TUI, Jet2, easyJet Holidays and similar).** 
TUI's all-inclusive packages include flights, accommodation, all meals, local drinks and airport transfers.
 
Most of its hotels also include kids' clubs and entertainment.
 
Its Holiday Villages and TUI MAGIC LIFE hotels are designed for families, with kids' clubs, swim academies and waterparks.

- **Turkey for value.** 
Flights from most UK airports take around five to six hours, and kids' clubs are well run.

- **Club Med.** 
Its kids' clubs take children from 4 months to 17 years, running 9:00am–5:30pm and again in the evenings.

- **Ikos (Greece and Spain), which is more upmarket.** 
Ikos Aria offers supervised activities for ages six months to 17, with a crèche for babies and younger children.
 
At Ikos Oceania in Halkidiki, the crèche, kids club and teens' club are all complimentary.


## 2. UK hotels with free childcare
These are good if you want to avoid flying. Meals usually aren't all included, but you eat at the hotel restaurants rather than cooking.

- **Luxury Family Hotels group.** 
New Park Manor in the New Forest gives two hours of free childcare in Four Bears Den for every night you stay.
 Fowey Hall in Cornwall is another. 
Its Ofsted-registered crèche takes babies from 12 weeks.

- **Calcot Manor, Cotswolds.** 
It offers four hours of complimentary childcare per day, which is significantly more than most.

- **Other names that come up.** 
Chewton Glen is a luxury option, Bedruthan and Watergate Bay are coastal, and Another Place is in the Lake District.
 
Gleneagles is recommended if childcare is your priority.


Book childcare sessions early. 
They fill quickly during school holidays.
 Free sessions are often tied to the number of nights you stay or to booking direct, so check the terms.

## 3. Other options
- **Luxury hotel specialists.** 
Mr & Mrs Smith lists stays with free childcare, and it has sections for kids' clubs and nannies.
 Away with the Kids also curates family hotels with childcare.
- **Villa or resort with a private nanny or chef.** This costs more but gives you flexibility, especially with babies or toddlers on different schedules. I didn't search for specific providers, so I can't recommend any.
- **Grandparents or friends on a joint trip.** This is the cheapest way to share the load, though it isn't fully hands-off.

## Things to check
- **Age limits.** Many clubs start at 2–3 years, though some take babies from 3–12 weeks or 4–6 months.
- **Hours.** "Free" childcare can be anything from 90 minutes a day to four hours, so compare it with what you expect.
- **Meals.** Check whether the package is half board, full board or all-inclusive, and whether the kids' clubs include meals.
- **Qualifications.** In the UK, look for Ofsted-registered crèches. Abroad, ask about staff qualifications and ratios.
